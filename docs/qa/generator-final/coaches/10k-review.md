# Independent blind coaching review — 10K specialist

I independently reviewed all 20 complete initial plans (U01–U10, X and Y), covering 324 weeks and 2,268 day records. The assessment is from a 10K coaching perspective but uses each athlete's actual race distance when judging specificity. No version identity was inferred. No event adaptation was reviewed. The scores were authored from these supplied plans and public sources, without consulting other reviews or repository material.

## Method and locked rubric

Every day was inspected for prescribed distance, pace, rest/strength status, run/walk ratio, strides, workout structure, warmup/cooldown, work duration, recovery duration and mode, purpose/zone, and conditional instructions. Full day descriptions were read with repeated instruction text consolidated; structured sessions and presentation warnings were checked separately. Weekly distance, running-day distribution, cross-week Sunday/Monday adjacency, quality distribution, long-run growth, deloads, specific work and taper were considered together. Compact seven-day ledgers and an individual coaching observation for every week are in the JSON. Race distance is excluded when evaluating training peaks and pre-race taper mileage. Formal work minutes exclude strides, easy recovery and warm/cool distance; a low work total is not automatically a bad plan.

Each criterion is rated 0–10. The locked total is `2×fit + 2×quality + 1.5×progression + 1.5×recovery + specificity + taper + coherence`. Weights are respectively 20, 20, 15, 15, 10, 10, 10. Fit concerns the supplied base, continuity, break, experience and available days; quality concerns the actual work rather than its name; progression includes both volume and stimulus; recovery includes within-week and cross-week loading; specificity concerns sustainable event-relevant work; taper concerns work and volume reductions with useful retained intensity; coherence concerns executable instructions, truthful reference/goal framing and purpose consistency.

As qualitative anchors, 8 denotes strong, plausible coaching with material individualization still needed; 6 denotes a usable structure with meaningful weaknesses; 4 denotes a major mismatch or inadequate development. Decimal scores express judgment, not measured precision. Scores do not constitute an injury probability or a race-time forecast. Critical defects are separately recorded and are not cancelled by a respectable recovery/taper score. A technical label discrepancy is distinguished from a physical overload.

## Public primary sources consulted

- [B.A.A. official 10K training](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-10k/info-for-athletes/b-a-a-10k-training/). Its levelled plans demonstrate preparatory phases, event-paced intervals, sustained tempo work, easy running, recovery days and a light final phase. Level Two targets an existing 15–25 miles/week base. I use its session continuity and athlete-level distinction as coaching comparisons, not mandatory mileage/workout quotas. Its web schedule and linked PDF differ, so no exact row was treated as a universal prescription.
- [Hal Higdon, official novice 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/novice-10k/). A three-run novice pathway with rest and optional walking establishes that 40km/week is not a universal requirement for 10K completion. Completion and performance development need different judgments; the supplied return plan can be appropriately cautious while still lacking strong timed-race preparation.
- [Hal Higdon, official intermediate 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/intermediate-10k/). Its explanation distinguishes experienced runners from novices, alternates tempo/interval development, includes walking or jogging recoveries, conversational long runs and fatigue-sensitive extra rest. This supports judging actual work and the athlete's history instead of requiring two quality days for every runner. Walking recoveries are not inherently invalid.
- [B.A.A. official half-marathon training](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-half/info-for-athletes/boston-half-training/). Sustained HM work, controlled simulations and long runs provide event-specific comparisons. The review's preference for shorter jog breaks in some HM blocks is an inference about continuity, not a claim that one precise rest interval is physiologically necessary.
- [B.A.A. official marathon training](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-marathon/info-for-athletes/boston-marathon-training/). Its four-day novice level builds prolonged endurance with long runs reaching 16–18 miles. This is context for the exceptionally short delivered long exposure in U09, not a universal minimum-distance law.
- [Buist et al., original randomized novice-runner trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/). The graded program based on the 10% rule did not reduce injury incidence relative to the comparator. I therefore do not declare safety or failure from a weekly growth percentage alone, especially when returning from a deliberate deload.
- [Shepley et al., original taper study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1559951/). The small crossover study in trained male middle-distance runners supports retaining some intensity while reducing volume. Its time-to-fatigue result cannot be converted into a predicted road-race gain for these synthetic athletes.
- [Bosquet et al., taper meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/). The competitive-athlete evidence favours substantial volume reduction with maintained intensity/frequency. Its approximately two-week, 41–60% framework informs rather than dictates the score: race distance, starting load, small beginner volume and actual work dose matter.

Sources were retrieved through public web search on 2026-10-12. No proprietary implementation was assumed and no commercial coaching product was used as an undisclosed benchmark.

## Key coaching findings

The meaningful differences concern executable dose and recovery, not merely metadata. U05 X offers 4×1200m at current 10K rhythm with 60-second walking recovery in week 13, then 3×1600m with 75 seconds in week 14. Both contain 4.8km specific work and complement threshold development. U05 Y instead gives 3×1200m and 2×1600m with four-minute walks. Those sessions are not unsafe by definition, but are less persuasive sustained-10K preparation for the supplied experienced athlete. U05 X's growth from 45km/week to a 68.9km training peak remains substantial and needs response-based review even with deloads.

U04 shows a different problem shared by both sides: novice quality is sensibly introduced, yet week 3 divides only 1.8km threshold work between two separate sessions. At 5:16/km these are about 5.8 and 3.7 work minutes, each accompanied by 3.1km warmup and 2.7km cooldown. This is inefficient fragmentation, not an arithmetic error. A second prescribed quality day does not establish a sufficient developmental stimulus. Y's later one-minute recoveries improve the 10K-rhythm blocks compared with X's nearly four-minute walks; the work total remains modest on both sides.

U06 Y appropriately acknowledges the year-old PB as a historical reference, makes easy days conversational, and supplies per-day effort guidance and a logged-tolerance condition for demanding work. X's general warning to check form is less actionable while immediately adding strides in the return phase and later showing fixed old-result speeds. Y's 75% completion, pain/effort and 90-day-test numbers are useful operational heuristics, not validated universal readiness cutoffs. Neither side's 6.7km peak long run and 18.2km/week training peak establish a strong timed-10K preparation. That judgment follows the delivered sustained exposure and return history, not the plan's internal 40km/week warning threshold.

U01 X makes the continuous-running transition a trial, with a concrete repeat-run/walk instruction if prior efforts were not conversational or pain-free. Y introduces continuous running and strides together on the calendar without equivalent day-level transition guidance. This materially affects fit, progression and recovery judgments. Low weekly mileage itself is not grounds to deny a novice 5K completion pathway.

The experienced 5K and half-marathon profiles also differ in actual development. U03 Y's roughly 14–21-minute threshold work and greater interval volume better use the established base than X's repeated two-rep plateau. U08 Y introduces sustained threshold and short-recovery HM blocks earlier, then reduces their work during taper. U08 X has useful endurance and simulation days but tiny early threshold doses and relatively high work retained during the taper. U07 X also begins HM specificity earlier, uses brief jogging recovery, and limits formal quality to one day in several long-run-building weeks. These judgments are event-dependent: a three-minute fartlek may be appropriate introduction for a returning runner and underdevelopment for an experienced performance-focused runner.

Many race-week sessions physically run near current event rhythm but are labelled repetitions/R in one side. The correction to explicit controlled activation improves purpose and coherence, particularly when the athlete might interpret a repetition label as permission to sprint. The physically prescribed dose is mild; the discrepancy is not by itself an acute-overload finding. Likewise a correctly classified workout can remain too small, too interrupted or poorly progressed to meet its coaching purpose.

## Critical defects separated from score

U09 X and U09 Y each receive a critical structural-preparation finding. The complete 20-week plan still ends with a 42.195km race after maximum training long exposure of 17.7km, approximately 105 minutes. Peak non-race mileage is 49.1km/week, and regular marathon-rhythm sessions are only about 2.2–2.5km work. The plan explicitly admits limited endurance and advises revising base/days/date. That honest warning improves coherence, but does not convert the delivered schedule into adequate standalone marathon preparation. I would not approve either as a complete race-ready marathon plan without changing its scope and making a concrete individualized race decision. This is not a prediction that injury must occur, nor a categorical rule that four running days cannot prepare a marathon.

No other critical defect was established from the supplied initial plans. Other material shortcomings are retained as coaching concerns rather than inflated into categorical safety claims. In particular low-volume beginner/return plans can be sensible if their completion decisions, effort guidance and tolerance rules are honored.

## Limits and interpretation

These are synthetic profiles. Age, injury and medical history, habitual day arrangement, actual recent session logs, sleep, work stress, hills/surface, climate, strength-training dose, fueling tolerance and current exertion response are absent. An indicated Sunday long run does not establish how Monday's strides will be tolerated. A requested weekly distance and a recent longest run do not prove that either is repeatable. Claimed continuity is athlete information, not verified adherence to these new plans.

Paces and modeled reference times are coaching inputs, not measurements of present physiology or forecasts of improvement. Warnings appropriately preserve the difference between a desired goal and a result-derived reference. Numerical readiness gates and 30/40/55km internal volume thresholds are not universal scientific boundaries. Recovery reductions must be compared with comparable build weeks; a rebound after deload is not automatically a dangerous spike. Taper assessment excludes the race itself, so U09/U10 race-week totals around 52.7km are not falsely treated as failed tapering.

No detailed nutrition efficacy score was assigned: fueling practice instructions were inspected as elements of specificity/coherence, but individual requirements are unknown. Sources provide illustrative coaching models and limited-population evidence, not proof that a particular runner needs one exact work duration, recovery interval, longest run or taper. Static instructions for conditional progression deserve credit, but I did not assert that the condition is enforced by unseen software. Event adaptation is deliberately outside this initial review.
